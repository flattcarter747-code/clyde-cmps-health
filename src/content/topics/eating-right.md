## What does "eating right" mean?

Eating right doesn't mean a strict diet or never having dessert. It means **most of the time**, choosing real, whole foods that give your body what it needs:

- **Vegetables and fruits** (fresh, frozen, or canned)
- **Protein**, like beans, eggs, chicken, fish, and meat
- **Dairy**, like milk, yogurt, and cheese
- **Whole grains**, like oatmeal, brown rice, and whole-wheat bread
- **Water** as your main drink

The newest U.S. **Dietary Guidelines for Americans (2025–2030)** sum it up as "eat real food." That means choosing foods close to how they grow, and cutting back on highly processed snacks, sweets, and sugary drinks.

## Why it matters

What we eat affects almost every health topic on this site, including **weight, blood pressure, blood sugar,** and **cholesterol**. Eating well can also help your mood, your energy, and how well you sleep.

Many families in our survey asked about **healthy meal planning**. You don't need fancy ingredients. Planning a few simple meals ahead of time is one of the best ways to save money and eat better.
